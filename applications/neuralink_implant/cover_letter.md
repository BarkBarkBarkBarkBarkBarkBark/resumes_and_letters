Dear Brain Interfaces Software Team,

I am applying for the Software Engineer Intern, Implant position in South San Francisco. I am a Biomedical Engineering M.S. student at UC Davis, where I develop Python software for human intracranial electrophysiology in Dr. Jie Zheng’s lab at UC Davis Health. Your team’s work on implant recording, manufacturing acceptance, and design-control verification is particularly appealing because I want to make neural acquisition systems dependable beyond the research lab.

My recent work has concentrated on the problems that arise between a recording and trustworthy downstream software. I implemented a Neo-backed adapter that allows SpikeInterface to read Neuralynx MED sessions despite malformed channel metadata. I also evaluated source-to-MED numerical fidelity over 20 million samples from one channel at 32 kHz: the largest absolute difference was 0.5 µV, and the compared arrays matched exactly after rounding the source to whole microvolts. This was a bounded data-conversion check—not a claim of full-system or clinical validation—but it reflects how I approach verification: define the comparison, measure discrepancies, and make limitations explicit.

I have also built Linux- and Python-based acquisition orchestration and replay tooling, including session configuration and archived-recording UDP replay. In another software project, I traced an approximately 70-second operation listing seven records to redundant schema validation, then introduced content-based caching and regression checks. Together these projects have taught me to diagnose interfaces and failure modes, not merely produce a successful demonstration.

I would value the opportunity to contribute to reliable implant software, test infrastructure, and recording tools while learning the rigor of production medical-device engineering. I can take a three-month leave of absence from my current position for a full-time internship and would be glad to discuss scheduling and on-site availability.
